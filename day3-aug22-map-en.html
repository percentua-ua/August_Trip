Day 3 — Saturday 22 August

Drive out at 08:15, parked in Vienna by 10:45, and then the only good-weather afternoon you get: Naschmarkt and its Saturday-only flea market, the Innere Stadt on foot, a free vintage-car parade on the Ring — and an evening in the Prater, where those same cars finish.

Reading the map, and lighting up one leg at a time. Dashed = you walk, and each walking leg has its own colour so consecutive legs never blur into one another — the badge on the line matches its colour and names the two stops it joins. Solid = you ride, in the transport line's real colour — and now badged exactly the same way: the line, and the two stops that leg joins.
Click any line and it lights up on its own — everything else dims, the map zooms to it, and a bar appears top-left with a show the whole day button. Click the same line again to undo it.
Click a numbered stop in the list and every leg touching that stop lights up — so clicking 11 shows you how you arrive at 11 and how you leave it, together.
Two things decide today. 1 — the garage height: the Prado is ~1.9 m and Vienna Tiefgaragen are commonly signed 1.80–2.00 m. Confirm the Durchfahrtshöhe at Reschgasse before you leave Budapest, and keep the free-street fallback in mind. 2 — the Austrian vignette (10-day, €12.80) must be live before you touch the A4.
You do not legally need to pay for parking this weekend. Vienna's Kurzparkzone runs Monday to Friday only. You arrive Saturday, leave Monday 06:00, and Monday's charge doesn't restart until 09:00 — so the kerb is free the whole time. The garage buys certainty, security and shade, not compliance. Reschgasse itself is a shopping street, which does charge Sat 08:00–12:00 on the street — so if you go for the kerb, use Mandlgasse.
Breakfast, twice, both markets. No hotel breakfast either end, so: 07:00 the Great Market Hall in Budapest for coffee and pastries from the bakery counters (Fakanál's hot breakfast doesn't open until 09:00, so it's standing up), then 12:00 the Naschmarkt in Vienna for the proper meal. Two of Europe's good markets in one morning, and the second one has the Saturday flea market attached.
If Vienna turns hot: Mariahilfer Straße is the city's big shopping street with Gerngross and Steffl on it, all air-conditioned and open Saturday until ~18:00 — and it runs between the MuseumsQuartier and Westbahnhof, so it is a short walk off your Innere Stadt loop. Everything on it is shut Sunday. Vienna is only 26 °C though, so this is a wet-weather or tired-feet option more than a heat one.

Today is the only chance for two things. The Naschmarkt flea market runs Saturdays only, and the whole market is closed Sunday. And every shop in Austria shuts tomorrow — buy water, breakfast fallbacks and the food for Monday's 1,000 km drive today.
Tickets, and why the usual advice is wrong. The 48- and 72-hour tickets were abolished on 1 January 2026. Buy two 24-hour tickets each (€10.20) at Niederhofstraße, set the second to start 24 h later, and stamp each one in the blue box at first use. Unstamped is a €105 fine. Toilets here run about €0.50 and are better than Budapest's.
What today costs, per person. Vienna tickets €20.40 for both days · Naschmarkt lunch ~€18 · one attraction if you take it €9–21 · dinner at the Schweizerhaus in the Prater ~€20–30. Classic Days, the Wurstelprater, Stephansdom's nave, the Hofburg courtyards and the Graben are all free. Roughly €60–80 a head, plus the garage and the vignette per car. Prices are estimates except the tickets and the vignette.
