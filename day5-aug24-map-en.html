Day 5 — Monday 24 August

The Bratislava morning only. Out of Vienna at 06:00, the empty old town at 07:10, the castle gates at 08:00 while it is still empty, the whole old town on foot by 08:45 — and away by 09:00. You eat once, in the room before you leave Vienna. The long drive home after that is deliberately not drawn here.

Leave Vienna at 06:00 and leave Bratislava by 09:00. Those two times are the whole discipline of the morning. Two hours here is enough for the castle and the old town; three starts eating the daylight you need for the Carpathian passes at the far end of the day, and that is the one safety margin in the plan.
One meal, in the room, bought on Saturday. Nothing in Austria is open at 05:15, and Sunday every shop in the country is shut — so Monday's breakfast gets bought two days earlier, at a supermarket on Meidlinger Hauptstraße.
For three: bread or rolls, butter, cheese and ham, yoghurt, fruit, coffee, and 3–4 litres of water for the car. About €15–20, against €30–40 sat down.
Eat 05:15–05:45, away by 06:00. That is the whole reason to do it in the room.
So Bratislava is sightseeing, not eating: old town 45 min, castle 40 min, and a coffee to go if you want one. If you would rather eat there, the three best rooms are pinned — but at two hours you can have the castle and a sit-down meal, or the castle and the old town, not all three.
Bratislava costs you almost nothing. It is 65 km from Vienna, and from here it is only 25 km south to Rajka and onto the Hungarian motorway — about 30 km and 25 minutes more than driving straight to Hegyeshalom. For that you get a castle at dawn, a deserted old town and a coronation cathedral, for about €10 all in.
Old town first, castle second — and that order is not a preference. The castle grounds open at 08:00 (daily 08:00–22:00, confirmed with the Bratislava Tourist Board), so arriving at the gate at 07:10 would just mean standing outside it. The old town streets are open all night and are genuinely deserted at 07:10, which is the better half of the trade. You reach the castle terrace at opening with nobody on it, and the only climb sits in the middle of the walk rather than at the start.
The Historical Museum inside costs €14 a head and opens at 10:00 (daily except Tuesday, so it would be open on your Monday). You are gone by then — skip it. The terrace and the Baroque Garden are the point and both are free.
What the Bratislava morning actually costs the three of you: €10–23, and the floor is €0.
Slovak e-vignette — €8.10 for 1 day, €10.80 for 10 days (official prices, eznamka.sk). Only needed if you touch a D-motorway. You can come in from Kittsee–Jarovce and leave south through Rusovce and Čunovo on ordinary roads and pay nothing — but buy the 1-day one online the night before and stop thinking about it.
Parking, 2 hours — €0 to €3. Gondova lot in the centre by the Danube: €0.50/30 min before 08:00, €1/30 min after, so 07:00–09:00 is €3.00. Tyršák 1 on the Petržalka bank is free from 03:00 but adds a bridge crossing on foot. PAAS street parking in the old town is €1.50–2/hour on a weekday. All surface — no height barrier for the Prado.
Castle grounds, Baroque Garden, terraces, Michael's Gate, Main Square, Čumil, St Martin's from outside — all free. There is no ticket to buy anywhere in this morning.
Optional: coffee to go €3–4 each (€9–12 for three), public toilet €0.50–1 a head.
Slovakia is euro — your EUR cards spend with zero conversion, the only place on this trip where that is true. Pay for everything by card; you need no Slovak cash at all. The Hungarian vignette from 20 August is still valid and the Austrian one has days left.
Everything after Bratislava is deliberately not on this map. In one line: Rajka at 10:10, onto the M1, round Budapest on the M0 southern ring, M3 to Vásárosnamény, cross at Beregsurány, freshest driver on the Mukachevo–Stryi passes (two and a half hours, not the ninety minutes a map promises), dinner in Lviv, home about 22:45. Fill up in Hungary, not Austria.
