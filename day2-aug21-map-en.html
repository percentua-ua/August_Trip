Reading the map, and lighting up one leg at a time. Dashed = you walk, and each walking leg has its own colour so consecutive legs never blur into one another — the badge on the line matches its colour and names the two stops it joins. Solid = you ride, in the transport line's real colour — and now badged exactly the same way: the line, and the two stops that leg joins.
Click any line and it lights up on its own — everything else dims, the map zooms to it, and a bar appears top-left with a show the whole day button. Click the same line again to undo it.
Click a numbered stop in the list and every leg touching that stop lights up — so clicking 11 shows you how you arrive at 11 and how you leave it, together.
Today is not a working day in Hungary. 21 August is an áthelyezett pihenőnap — a transferred rest day — so the whole country is off Thursday to Sunday. Consequences: every popular sight has the domestic long-weekend crowd, BKK runs a weekend timetable so add 10 minutes to each hop, and offices and banks are shut. Shops do open, on roughly Saturday hours.
The 09:15 queue is the whole plan. Parliament's free open day has no ticket, no registration and no time slot — you turn up and queue, and so does the rest of the country on a day off. Airport-style security, no large bags and no cloakroom, so leave everything at the hotel. Sealed non-glass bottles are fine, and you'll want one. Listings disagree on whether it closes at 16:00 or 18:00 — irrelevant if you're in the queue at 09:15.
The afternoon is built around water, not shade. Ruin bars at 15:45, then the Rudas rooftop pool from 17:00 — you are out of the street heat and in the Danube view at the same time. Gellért Hill is deliberately at 19:15, when the air finally drops, and Rudas sits at the bottom of it.
Four malls are now pinned as cold-air escapes, all open today on Saturday-style hours (they were shut yesterday for the holiday): Westend at Nyugati is the best placed, 900 m from Oktogon · Mammut at Széna tér is the Buda one, 15 min from the Vienna Gate if the Castle gets you · Corvin Plaza is closest to the hotel · Arena is the furthest and probably unnecessary. All four give you free clean toilets, free wifi, ATMs and a supermarket — which solves the 200-forint-coin problem for an hour.
And three free tricks: the M4 is air-conditioned, trains and stations both · Fakanál is the only cooled spot inside the Market Hall · every museum on the list is cold and dark. Refill bottles at the drinking fountains in City Park and on Castle Hill, and at the free water points the city set up at Keleti, Nyugati and Déli.
How to read the lines — it is now ONE unbroken chain from 0 to 17.
Dashed teal = you walk. Badged with the two stops it joins: 0→1, 2→3, and so on.
Solid colour = you ride, in the line's real colour, and badged the same way as the walking legs — the move, the line, and the two stops it joins: A1 M4 · 1→Kálvin, A2 M3 · Kálvin→Deák, A3 M2 · Deák→2, B funicular 7→8, C bus 7 13→14, E, F tram 47/49.
Follow the line and you can trace the whole day without guessing.

So, 1 → 2 in full: stop 1 is the station under the hotel. A1 M4 one stop east to Kálvin tér · change · A2 M3 two stops north-west to Deák Ferenc tér · change · A3 M2 one stop to Kossuth Lajos tér · then the dashed 1→2 leg, 150 m across the square to the queue. Three trains, two changes, one short walk, ~15 min — add 10 for the weekend timetable. It looks like a dog-leg on the map because it is one: you go east to Kálvin before turning north.

Where a tunnel is used twice (M3 and M4, morning and evening) the later leg is drawn 60 m to one side, so D1–D3 don't hide underneath A1–A3.

Three stations do all the work: Kálvin tér (M3/M4), Deák Ferenc tér (M1/M2/M3) and Fővám tér (M4, your door). And you pass Fővám tér again during D3 — drop the market shopping then, not later.
Eating today, without repeating yourself. 07:45 breakfast at the market — and do the gift shopping then, because you live 60 m away and can drop it in the room. That is the only reason the old plan came back here at lunchtime.
14:45 lunch: Belvárosi Disznótoros, a meat counter you point at, five minutes from where Elisabeth Bridge lands you. Cheap, fast, and what Hungarians actually eat. Alternatives: Gerlóczy for a calm sit-down, or Baltazár up on Castle Hill if you would rather eat before descending.
On the hill at ~13:00: Ruszwurm, open since 1827, thirty seconds from Matthias Church. Order the krémes.
20:15 dinner: Bartók Béla út, on the Buda side. 0.9 km downhill from the Citadella, twelve minutes — instead of a 45-minute return trek to District VII after a day with 11 km in it. Hadik Kávéház (no. 36) for a proper dinner, Szatyor Bár next door for a drink after, Kelet Kávézó (no. 29) for something lighter. Then 600 m to Szent Gellért tér and M4 one stop home. The District VII places are still pinned if you'd rather have the ruin bars.
Quick and cheap all day: Karaván food trucks, Bors GasztroBár, or a lángos.
Toilets. Same rules as yesterday: about 200 HUF, often coin-operated, carry 200-forint coins. Today you're better served than Thursday — the Market Hall is open, Parliament has facilities inside the visit, and City Park and the Castle both have public ones.
