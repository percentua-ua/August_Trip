Day 1 — Thursday 20 August

Reading the map, and lighting up one leg at a time. Dashed = you walk, and each walking leg has its own colour so consecutive legs never blur into one another — the badge on the line matches its colour and names the two stops it joins. Solid = you ride, in the transport line's real colour — and now badged exactly the same way: the line, and the two stops that leg joins.
Click any line and it lights up on its own — everything else dims, the map zooms to it, and a bar appears top-left with a show the whole day button. Click the same line again to undo it.
Click a numbered stop in the list and every leg touching that stop lights up — so clicking 11 shows you how you arrive at 11 and how you leave it, together.
Map apps won't know this. The Buda lower embankment is closed to cars 15–22 Aug, but it's temporary so routing sends you along the road above. Stay down by the water.
You can't also do the Holy Right Procession. It leaves St Stephen's Basilica at 19:00, 2.6 km away on the Pest side. Pick one: a good spot for the show, or the procession and a worse view.
Nothing on this map is shut today — I removed the pins that were. It's a national holiday, so Lidl, Aldi, SPAR, Penny, Tesco and Auchan are all closed, and so is the Great Market Hall across from your hotel — as are post offices and ordinary pharmacies (only the duty pharmacy, ügyeletes gyógyszertár, is open; look up the nearest one to the hotel before you travel). What is open: shops inside railway stations, petrol-station shops (SPAR Express / DESPAR), some non-stop convenience shops, some tobacconists, and every restaurant, café and bar. Etele Plaza, the big mall right at the Kelenföld P+R, is CLOSED today — malls shut for the national holiday, so don't count on it for air conditioning or supplies on arrival. Buy water and snacks at Kelenföld station when you park — it's a station, so it's open, and you walk right past it. Friday everything reopens.
No breakfast at the hotel — so the market is the plan, not the fallback. The Great Market Hall is directly across your street and opens at 06:00. It's shut today for the holiday, but from tomorrow it's your breakfast every morning: bakery counters downstairs, a lángos upstairs, coffee from the stands, 2,000–3,000 Ft a head — against €15–20 for a hotel buffet you aren't getting anyway. See Day 2 and Day 3 for the detail.
Tonight, eating is solved by the festival: the food street at Várkert Bazár runs 09:00–24:00, so stop 6 is dinner and there's no need to find a restaurant on the busiest night of the year. If you land hungry before 17:30, Cafe Brunch Budapest is 30 seconds from the door and open — cafés aren't affected by the holiday closure.
Euro → forint, and where to do it. Rate: about 362 HUF to €1 (the MNB benchmark sat at 360–364 in mid-2026 — check your phone on the day).
Seven ATMs are now pinned, numbered by usefulness. ① Kelenföld station hall is the one that matters — draw 60,000–80,000 Ft for all three of you in a single withdrawal while you're buying tickets. It's at the car, before the hotel, and station ATMs run on a public holiday when every counter in the city is shut. ② is the metro concourse next door if that machine is dry. ③ Fővám tér is under your bed, ④ Kálvin tér is the best cluster of real bank branches, ⑤ Batthyány tér is 78 m from tonight's viewing spot — but event-adjacent machines empty first on a night like this, so don't plan on it. ⑥ and ⑦ are marked verify: probably there, not guaranteed.
Changing euro notes: bureaux are shut Thursday, open Friday. Use the ones inland of Ferenciek tére, not on Váci utca.
Three rules: always choose HUF, never "charge in your home currency" · never the blue-and-yellow Euronet ATMs (2,000–3,000 Ft lost on a 50,000 withdrawal) · "0% commission" means the markup is in the rate — Hungarian law sets a 0.3% legal minimum, so nobody is doing it free.
What today actually costs, per person. Transport 5,750 HUF (the 72-hour card, covers everything) · dinner at the food street ~4,000 HUF · water and snacks from Kelenföld ~1,000 HUF · toilets 200 HUF a go, in coins · the show, the light painting, the disco, Elektro Rakpart and the island concerts: all free. Roughly 11,000 HUF / €30 a head for the whole day, plus ~6,300 HUF for the car across the three of you.
Cash rules: festival stands and market stalls are cash-first, and public toilets want 200-forint coins. The P+R is the opposite — card only, no cash accepted. Rate: about 362 HUF to €1 (MNB benchmark mid-2026 was 360–364) — check it on your phone before you change anything, and see the money pins. Prices are Aug 2026 estimates; the capped drink prices at the food street are official.
The show runs twice — so stop treating 21:00 as your only chance.
21:00–21:30 full show + the 10-min fireworks, from the quay.
21:30 leave while everyone else is still standing there. Walk south down the car-free quay.
22:00–22:30 the entire show is repeated (no fireworks, no drones) — watch it from further down the same bank with a fraction of the crowd.
22:30–01:00 light painting on Parliament and the Chain Bridge — and you walk directly under the Chain Bridge at stop 7 on the way home. The walk back is the last act.
Then pick one: home by ~23:15 · or over Liberty Bridge to Elektro Rakpart for the end of Modeselektor and Tolo & Falcao to 01:30, 11 min from your bed · or the Kossuth tér disco to 01:30, 15 min over the Chain Bridge · or the Fénykert on Margaret Island, open to 01:00.
Getting home — four ways, all on your travelcard. All of them start with an 80 m walk south to Batthyány tér.
A · 3 lines, 2 changes, ~20 min — M2 → M3 → M4, out at the hotel door. Least walking, worst crowds.
B · 2 lines, 1 change, ~20 min — M2 → M3, out at Kálvin tér, then 380 m / 5 min down Vámház körút. Same time as A with one less interchange — take this one.
C · 1 line, no changes, ~25 min — M2 only to Deák Ferenc tér, then 1.4 km / 17 min through the Belváros.
D · no metro at all, 3.3 km / ~41 min — back down the car-free quay and over Liberty Bridge to the door.
Metros run to about 02:30 tonight.
Toilets, the honest version. Public ones cost about 200 HUF and many are coin-operated — carry coins. They cluster in metro stations, pedestrian underpasses and markets; look for the WC pictogram. Buy a drink and use a café if you're stuck. On 20 Aug organisers put temporary toilets at every event site, including the food street and along the quays — those are your best bet all evening.
